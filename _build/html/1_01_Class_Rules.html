Class Rules#

AI#

When you do the exercises in this textbook you should NOT use AI.

Every line of code should be written by you, from your brain ✅🧠🙂 NOT from a robot brain ❌🤖.

If you use AI while you are learning to code for the first time, you will not learn how to code. You will not be able to debug and fix code when it goes wrong.

Have a look at this article which explains some of the bad effects of using AI when you are learning new coding skills. It says that:

  • using AI when you learn will make you faster to finish coding tasks…

  • …BUT you will not understand the code you write, and you will be slower to learn the new skills.

Thinking is hard. It takes effort. But, like exercising and eating healthily, it is good for you.

Computers can make us stupid (even without AI)#

Note

This section is adapted from Brett, Rush, Adhikari, Denero and Wagner (2023). See the license page for more information.

Computers let us do lots of things we could not do without them. But they can also distract us and make us dumb.

The computer has lots of nice colours, we can have 100s of browser tabs open, we can have Youtube playing as we write code and video call our friends. You get the idea.

Our computer can take up enough mental energy that we stop thinking carefully about the task we are doing. This is particularly dangerous when programming, because programming needs careful logical thought.

For example, Muller et al (2014) found that when students listened to a lecture, those who took notes on paper remembered more than those who took notes on a laptop.

As programmers, the students with the laptops are us. We have to be careful about the dumbing effect that using the computer can have, on our ability to think straight.

Experienced programmers know this problem, and they use tricks to deal with them.

Among these tricks, are the following.

Have a piece of paper and a pen next to you#

When working on the computer, have a piece of paper and a pen next to you on your desk.

When you notice that you don’t fully understand an error or a task on the computer, stop, move the laptop out of the way, and write down your problem on a piece of paper.

Give yourself some time to think about what the problem is, and how to solve it.

Only then, you can return to the computer and try your solutions, or do more research. You will find that disengaging from the computer is a) difficult and b) very productive in releasing you from the various mental traps that it is easy to fall into when you get stuck on the computer.

Working together in class#

When we are in class, you can talk to each other as your code. But you should write every line of code yourself (do not copy from others).

Again, you must use must your own brain, not your friend’s brain, to write your code. As long as you do NOT copy any code, you can help each and support each other if you get stuck (there is a tip for how to do this in the next section).

What is OK:

  • Asking a friend for advice on how to fix a bug

  • Pair programming (see the next section)

Pair programming#

One way of working together that you can use when completing the exercises in this textbook is pair programming. Note: this is working together where you are both thinking about and writing code as a team, it is not one person copying someone else’s code, that is not allowed.

Pair programming is a technique that programmers use to improve the quality of their work and accelerate learning. It is particularly useful early in learning, and with new tasks(Lui & Chan, 2006).

Pair programming has a standard form.

One member of the team is the driver. They sit in front of the keyboard and type.

The other member of the team is the navigator. They do not use their own laptop, but watching the driver as they work, offering advice, and talking about ideas with the driver.

The pair should switch roles, for example, changing places for each exercise.

You will likely find that, when you are the driver, you think a lot slower than the navigator, because you are thinking about two things, the task, and interacting with the computer. When you switch to being the navigator, you will find that you can think more quickly and carefully.
